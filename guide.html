Buying guide

How to buy a classic car from Japan online

Twelve steps from choosing a car to looking after it in India. You can do them yourself, or have NHM handle everything except the purchase itself. The rules and rates change, so confirm each step with a licensed customs broker before you bid.

General guidance, not legal or tax advice. Based on India's vintage import policy as published in DGFT Notification 58/2024-25 (February 2025).

  1. Check the car is eligible

    India allows a car to be imported as vintage once it is 50 years old. The cutoff rolls forward, so each year another model year qualifies. Enter the year the car was built, or first registered if that's what your broker uses.

  2. Know the import rules

    Under the 2025 policy, a vintage car can be imported freely by its actual user, without an import licence. Plan around these conditions:

    • Import it in your own name. The policy is for the person who will own and use the car. That's why, with NHM, you are always the buyer.
    • It's for personal use. The policy restricts reselling imported vintage cars in India. Ask your broker what applies before you buy.
    • It must meet road rules to be driven. Vintage cars used on public roads must comply with the Motor Vehicles Act and be registered as vintage.
    • India drives on the left, like Japan, so Japanese-market cars are already right-hand drive.

  5. Register and pay a deposit

    Open an account with your agent or exporter. Agents usually ask for a refundable deposit before they will bid. Before you send money:

    • Check that the company is real: look for JUMVEA membership and a company bank account in its own name.
    • Agree the fees in writing: agent commission, auction fees, inland transport, export papers and shipping.
    • Confirm that they will ship to India and supply what your broker needs for a vintage import.

  7. Get an independent inspection

    An auction sheet is a quick check, not a survey. For a classic, pay for an inspector to see the car before you bid or buy.

    • Photographs of the underside, floors, sills and suspension mounts.
    • The chassis plate and engine number, to confirm the car is what it claims to be.
    • A cold start and a short video of the engine running.
    • Any service books, previous owners' records and the Japanese registration papers.

    Each listing has model-specific things to check.

  8. Win, pay and deregister

    If you win, you'll usually have a few working days to pay the balance by bank transfer. The seller then deregisters the car for export and issues an export certificate, the Japanese document that proves the car's identity and first registration. Keep the original safe; customs and your RTO will need it.

  9. Ship it to India

    • Container: the car is sealed in its own or a shared container. It costs more and is safer, so it's the usual choice for a valuable classic.
    • Roll-on, roll-off (RoRo): the car is driven onto a car carrier. It costs less, but the car must run and is more exposed.
    • Main ports for cars include Nhava Sheva (Mumbai), Chennai and Mundra. Choose one where your broker works.
    • Insure the car for its full value in transit, with marine insurance.

    With NHM: we arrange collection, export papers, container shipping and insurance once you have paid the seller. About shipping

  10. Clear customs

    Appoint a licensed customs broker at the port before the car sails. They file the bill of entry, assess duty and IGST, and arrange release. Typical documents:

    • Commercial invoice and proof of payment
    • Bill of lading and marine insurance certificate
    • Japanese export certificate, showing the car's age
    • Your identity documents, and anything else your broker asks for

    With NHM: we coordinate clearance with a licensed broker at the port.

  11. Register it as vintage

    To drive it on public roads, register the car as vintage with your state's transport authority under the Central Motor Vehicles Rules. Vintage cars get their own registration series and conditions of use. Take the customs papers, the export certificate and proof of the car's age.

    With NHM: we prepare the registration papers and take the car to your transport authority.

  12. Look after it

    A classic needs regular care from people who know it: fluids and rubber that age even when the car sits, carburettors that need setting up, and parts that often have to come from Japan. Plan for servicing before the car arrives.
