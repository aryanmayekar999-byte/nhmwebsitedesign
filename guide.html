Buying guide

How to buy a classic Japanese car

Twelve steps from choosing a car to looking after it in India. NHM is preparing a concierge service; the process below is for research until that service launches. Rules and rates change, so confirm each step with a qualified customs broker before you bid.

General guidance, not legal or tax advice. Read DGFT Notification 58/2024-25 and Central Motor Vehicles Rules amendment G.S.R. 492(E). Confirm current rules with a qualified broker and transport authority.

  1. Check the car is eligible

    Under the cited vintage rules, the specific car must be more than 50 years old from its first registration after first sale, remain in original form without substantial overhaul, and meet other conditions. Enter a model year for an initial screen only; verify the car's documents with a qualified broker before buying.

  2. Know the import rules

    The cited 2025 DGFT notification allows qualifying vintage cars to be imported by actual users under its stated exemption. Confirm the current classification and every condition for the specific car before purchase:

    • Import it in your own name. The policy is for the person who will own and use the car. That's why, with NHM, you are always the buyer.
    • It's for personal use. The vintage rules restrict use and transfers; confirm the current conditions with your broker and registering authority before buying.
    • It must meet road rules to be driven. Vintage vehicles have specific registration and permitted-use conditions; do not assume ordinary daily road use is allowed. Confirm with the registering authority.
    • Expect right-hand drive, then verify it. NHM focuses on Japanese-market cars and Japanese classics sold into right-hand-drive markets such as Australia and New Zealand. Check the specific car’s steering layout and registration requirements before committing, especially when sourcing through a market that drives on the right.

  3. Work out the landed cost

    Use this worksheet to plan cash needed across the purchase, transport and arrival stages. Enter your agent’s and broker’s written quotes. Cars may be sourced from Japan, the Gulf, Australia, New Zealand or other markets. Enter all purchase and shipping amounts in the selected currency; convert any separately quoted USD freight or other charges into that currency first. Customs valuation and tax depend on the specific vehicle and the assessment at import; ICEGATE’s duty calculator and your broker are the sources for a tax figure.

  5. Register and pay a deposit

    Open an account with your agent or exporter. Some agents request a bidding deposit; refund and forfeiture rules vary. Get those terms in writing before you send money:

    • Check that the company is real: look for JUMVEA membership and a company bank account in its own name.
    • Agree the fees in writing: agent commission, auction fees, inland transport, export papers and shipping.
    • Confirm that they will ship to India and supply what your broker needs for a vintage import.

  6. Read the auction sheet

  7. Get an independent inspection

    An auction sheet is a quick check, not a survey. For a classic, pay for an inspector to see the car before you bid or buy.

    • Photographs of the underside, floors, sills and suspension mounts.
    • The chassis plate and engine number, to confirm the car is what it claims to be.
    • A cold start and a short video of the engine running.
    • Any service books, previous owners' records and the Japanese registration papers.

    Each listing has model-specific things to check.

    With NHM: we arrange the inspection and send you the report before you commit. About inspections

  8. Win, pay and deregister

    If you win, you'll usually have a few working days to pay the balance by bank transfer. The seller then deregisters the car for export and issues an export certificate, the Japanese document that proves the car's identity and first registration. Keep the original safe; customs and your RTO will need it.

  9. Ship it to India

    • Container: the car is sealed in its own or a shared container. It costs more and is safer, so it's the usual choice for a valuable classic.
    • Roll-on, roll-off (RoRo): the car is driven onto a car carrier. It costs less, but the car must run and is more exposed.
    • Main ports for cars include Nhava Sheva (Mumbai), Chennai and Mundra. Choose one where your broker works.
    • Insure the car for its full value in transit, with marine insurance.

    With NHM: we arrange collection, export papers, container shipping and insurance once you have paid the seller. About shipping

  10. Clear customs

    Appoint a licensed customs broker at the port before the car sails. They file the bill of entry and help calculate expected charges; customs determines the assessment and release. Typical documents:

    • Commercial invoice and proof of payment
    • Bill of lading and marine insurance certificate
    • Japanese export certificate, showing the car's age
    • Your identity documents, and anything else your broker asks for

    With NHM: we coordinate clearance with a licensed broker at the port.

  11. Register it as vintage

    To drive it on public roads, register the car as vintage with your state's transport authority under the Central Motor Vehicles Rules. Vintage cars get their own registration series and conditions of use. Take the customs papers, the export certificate and proof of the car's age.

    With NHM: we prepare the registration papers and take the car to your transport authority.

  12. Look after it

    A classic needs regular care from people who know it: fluids and rubber that age even when the car sits, carburettors that need setting up, and parts that often have to come from Japan. Plan for servicing before the car arrives.
